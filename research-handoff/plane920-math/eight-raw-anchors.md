# Eight helpers and eight ORs force three to five distinct raw-AND anchors

Assume a terminal-normalized candidate for the thirteen-line problem with eight hidden ANDs and eight physical ORs. The normalization of seven-raw-ports.md applies without change. In particular each terminal can be narrowed to a fixed valid pair of raw/helper atoms with no changes to the input wires of any hidden gates. A forced low bare target is a line for which some p in L is invisible at the hidden wires under the change L to L minus p. Its terminal must directly read p and its other atom is a helper true on the triple L minus p. Different forced (point,line) obligations either yield different targets with distinct low helpers, or are impossible when two different points require the same target's unique raw atom.

## Positive-set and point-deletion budgets

Any two helpers cover at least five named positive rows in their union: otherwise their removal leaves six helpers for nine targets. Any three have union at least nine because five helpers cannot handle five complete targets. Any four have union at least eleven because four helpers cannot realize three. The same statements apply to the *novel* positive-row sets relative to the OR of all raw singletons accepted by each helper. This OR is a pointwise lower bound for the helper. Replacing several helpers by these raw ORs simultaneously preserves every target positive row outside the union of the novel sets, and can only shrink outputs on negative inputs. Free OR is allowed in each invoked subfamily lower bound. Fixed mandatory signals may additionally be merged if they compute exactly the same function after a common restriction; preserving the earlier physical occurrence avoids any dependency cycle.

Every raw p can directly feed at most one distinct hidden AND. Two such ANDs would be zero on all nine complete lines not containing p, which six remaining helpers cannot realize. In fact any helper whose true positive rows all belong to the pencil of p has the same uniqueness property. A hidden AND physically fed by such a gate cannot feed another hidden AND directly, since any AND consuming its value would also imply membership in the same pencil. Here the first phrase means the pencil-constrained gate itself has no hidden AND consumer. It may feed ORs or terminals.

If the first four gate positions are being studied individually, a low bare partner appearing as the second or third AND has exactly one true target line, by the local triple/quartet bounds in six-helper-proof.md. This entire case is incompatible with the eight-helper budgets. rare-early.js fixes its bare point p=0 and the target L={0,1,3,9}, by point-line flag symmetry. The rare gate accepts only the triple L minus p on all three-subsets, rejects all other named four-sets, and rejects every other four-set containing p. The first ANDs are ordinary ANDs of unrestricted ORs over raw points and earlier helpers. Each pair of their novel sets must have union of size at least five and each triple at least nine. The two- and three-AND instances were independently replayed (1196 and 7327 RUP additions). A rank-four control allowing extra true target lines through a fixed residual point was SAT in the same relaxation. The first AND cannot be a low bare partner because it has a singleton or pair witness. Therefore at most five low bare targets are possible, using positions four through eight.

## The geometry of the direct raw ANDs

A pure raw AND(p,q) is true on only the line through p and q. There cannot be two other hidden ANDs that directly read some raw point: those two have combined support within two four-line pencils, whose union has size at most seven, so together with the pure AND the three cover at most eight lines. Thus a pure raw AND would leave at most one other hidden AND with a raw input. Two pure raw ANDs are also impossible by the two-helper support floor. In the pure-AND case let d be the number of hidden AND raw input ports. Then d is at most three.

For h=8 let t be the number of raw input ports of the hidden ORs, let u be the number of raw points absent from the entire hidden network, and let a be the number of singly-read raw points which enter an AND. There are at least 2(13-u)-t-d singly-read points overall. At most 16-t are singly-read at a mixed OR, so at least 10-2u-d-a enter a pure raw OR. Each forces one bare low partner via the projective line through the two OR operands. Each unread point forces four. Let b count the singly-read inputs of a pure AND gate when it exists. Each such point additionally forces three bare low partners on the lines through it other than the common line with the second AND input. Thus there must be at least 10+2u-d-a+3b low bare targets. If there is a pure raw AND then a is at most b+1. The lower bound is at least six, contradicting the five-slot upper bound. Hence there is no pure raw AND.

Every remaining direct raw AND reads just one raw point, and all these points are distinct. Write d for their count. The bound without pure ANDs is 10+2u-d-a, with a at most d. The five-slot upper bound forces d at least three. On the other hand, if four direct points had no three collinear, their four pencils would cover only ten lines, contradicting the eleven-line floor for their four ANDs. Thus every four direct points have a collinear triple. Such a point set lies in one line plus at most one other point: if it contains a triangle, all extra points are on triangle sides, and points in the interiors of two different sides together with the two opposite vertices give a four-point cap. There are at most five direct points.

For d=3, u must be zero, and at least two of the direct raw inputs are singly read. A singly-read p entering H=p AND W further forces one distinct low bare target for each p-line on which H is false. On the other lines through p the signal may be sensitive to p. The same counting yields

  low bare targets >= 10 + 2u - d - a + sum_{p singly read at H} (4 - number of true p-lines of H).

Consequently if a=2 both direct gates are true on their entire four-line pencils. If a=3 the three together miss at most one line from their respective pencils. For each singly-read full-pencil p, the other input W has no dependency on p and must be true on the four pairwise disjoint triples obtained by removing p from its lines.

These conditions do not rule out the three to five direct points and do not prove the eight-helper/eight-OR case.
