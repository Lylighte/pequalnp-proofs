# Free-OR lower bounds after deleting plane targets

Use the dual, terminal, and low/high conventions of six-helper-proof.md. A target family is any subset of the thirteen indexed lines. Coordinates outside a chosen line union can always be fixed to false. A bare terminal is a raw point paired with a helper. A helper-pair terminal uses two different helpers. All true raw partners have a private point on the positive support of their helper.

## Three local rank bounds

Let g supply a bare terminal for the line L with point p. If g is low, its sole true input of size at most three is the triple T = L minus p. With at most three ANDs g cannot be true on any other line, by the disjoint and single-intersection cases of triple-quartet.js. With four ANDs its true lines are contained in a single pencil, by the disjoint triple/four-set and two-four-set argument in six-helper-proof.md.

Suppose g is high. For any other true line M, p does not lie on M. If two other true lines M,N intersect L at different points, L,M,N have three different pairwise intersections. Their union is a nine-point triangle. The marker p on L is one of its two points outside M and N; these two choices are isomorphic by interchanging raw coordinates. A circuit for g must be true on the three four-sets, false on all inputs of size at most three, and false on any other four-set containing p. triangle-onemark.js encodes this on the union only. Arbitrary ORs before and between ANDs are allowed. Four ANDs are UNSAT (10956 RUP additions); five are UNSAT as well (170314). Thus through rank five every high bare partner has all its true target lines through one point, at most four. The case of at most one other true line also satisfies this pencil conclusion.

With four ANDs a high bare partner cannot even be true on three concurrent lines. The union has ten points, the marker p is off the common intersection and occurs exclusively in its own line. concurrent-onemark.js replayed 8890 RUP additions at four ANDs, while five ANDs are SAT. Together with the triangle test, a rank-four high bare partner has at most two true target lines. two-marked.js excludes one bare mark and a second true line through rank three (336 RUP additions), and excludes two marks on two lines through rank four (7052 additions, SAT at rank five). All these small encoders use only the two operand-symmetry lexicographic ordering, ordinary unrestricted OR selectors, and inclusion-maximal negative evaluation rows. Maximal negative rows suffice by monotonicity. A smaller circuit can duplicate its output to reach the asserted rank.

## Every six complete targets need six helpers

Suppose a family of six targets has only five helpers. Every helper is active on at least four target rows, since the three complete targets missed by a helper would have only four helpers. Any two helpers therefore co-occur on at least two of the six rows, so none supplies a helper-pair terminal. The first three helpers cannot be bare partners, as either low or high rank-three partners have at most one true line. Of the fourth helpers, a high fourth helper has at most two true lines when used as a bare partner, while a low fourth helper supplies at most one bare terminal. The fifth helper supplies at most one bare terminal if low; if high, it supplies at most four, since its supporting lines belong to one pencil. At most five of the required six terminals can be supplied. Seven or more complete targets inherit this lower bound by restriction.

## Every ten complete targets need seven helpers

Assume six helpers realize an arbitrary ten-line family. Each helper must be active on at least five of the ten lines: otherwise fix it to false on the six missed target lines and use the preceding result. Any pair of helpers is jointly absent on at most two lines, and any triple jointly absent on at most one, as in six-helper-proof.md. The rank bounds above show that none of the first four helpers can supply a bare terminal, and the fifth can do so only if low, for at most one target. Hence only the sixth helper can supply multiple bare terminals. Such a helper is high.

The graph method in pair-edges-partial.js uses the ten target rows, six vertices, and exactly the same joint-absence bounds as the thirteen-line proof. It gives at most four helper-pair terminals. Every four-edge survivor is a four-leaf star; its thirty labeled graphs form one orbit. This can be checked with node pair-edges-partial.js 10; small-core/pair-edges-10.json preserves the graph masks and outcomes. At least six of the ten targets must therefore be bare. At least five of these come from the sixth helper H. Its positive set A has size at most six (see the private-point table). In particular A has at most one row beyond those assigned a raw terminal using H.

The sixth helper H cannot occur in any helper-pair terminal. Any partner C for such a terminal must be false on all the at least five target rows assigned a bare terminal using H. On those five targets deleting C preserves H's value and keeps all five bare pairs correct. The resulting H is still high and is computed by at most five ANDs. Its five true targets cannot belong to one four-line pencil, contradicting the rank-five marked-triangle result.

Four helper-pair terminals are impossible. In their common-center star all four respective edge rows make the center true. Each leaf is false on the other three edge rows, or else its conjunction with the common center would activate the wrong output. At most one edge row is in A. If exactly one is in A, choose the leaf for this row; if none is in A, choose any leaf. Its three required false rows all lie outside A. This contradicts the joint-absence budget of two for this leaf and H, which are different helpers by the preceding paragraph.
If there are three or fewer helper-pair terminals, there must be exactly three, with H supplying exactly six bare terminals and the fifth (low) helper supplying the seventh. The positive set A of H is exactly its six marked lines. For each earlier helper g, its false lines within A must all lie in one pencil. Otherwise choose three nonconcurrent such lines, delete g, and retain H and the three marked bare terminals. This gives the impossible five-AND marked triangle. Thus for an earlier helper pair to be a terminal outside A, their two false sets must cover A with two pencils. The direct check six-private-pencils.js scans all six-line sets whose six members have private points. There are 234 such sets and the union of any two pencils covers at most five members in every case. This rules out all three proposed pair terminals.

At eleven or twelve target lines, six helpers are already impossible: each helper is true on at least n-5 rows by the six-target bound, so at eleven or twelve the rank and seven-bare ceilings still apply. Twelve targets need at least eight raw terminals. Eleven need seven, forcing the sixth helper to supply six and hence to have no excess positive row for a pair. Four pair terminals would all lie outside A, so no star vertex could be H and the joint-absence contradiction applies directly. The complete independently replayed twelve-target 43-orbit check is also under small-core/partial-cnf.

The ten-line argument includes the case where H has a sixth true line outside its five assigned bare terminals.

## Every five complete targets need six helpers

Assume five helpers for any five retained lines. Every helper must be active on at least three of these positive rows, by the three-line/five-helper bound. Every pair of helpers is jointly absent on at most one of these rows, by the two-line/four-helper bound. The first three helpers cannot supply a bare pair since rank-three bare partners have at most one positive line. The fourth can be a bare partner only when low, then on at most one target, with all its true lines lying in a pencil. The fifth can be low with at most one bare target, or high with all its true rows in a pencil. A repeated endpoint is treated as bare.

The first two helpers also cannot form an endpoint pair. On their accepting row L a consistent proof of a conjunction of the first two helpers has four distinct essential raw leaves and just three available ANDs including the terminal. Its proof is consequently a tree with the two helpers taking two disjoint raw source pairs, and the second helper can be made true using its raw pair C. Each minimum witness of the first helper has size at most two. Its union with C must equal L, so every such witness is the same pair L minus C. As the first helper is an AND of raw ORs, every input on which it is true contains some such witness. It can therefore be true only on the one plane line containing that pair, contradicting its three active rows.

The finite check five-target-signatures.js partitions sets of five target lines by the incidence-preserving frame maps. The three representatives 31, 87, 143 have orbit sizes 702, 468, 117. For each signature five-tuple, any line whose two-helper pair cannot be isolated among the positive signatures must be assigned an allowed bare partner with a private point. The upper bound on raw targets owned by the fifth helper is one unless its true lines lie in a pencil. The fourth helper's upper bound is always one and its true lines must lie in a pencil to be used. This stage checks only signatures and is a relaxation.

five-partial.js extends a fixed prefix of the ordered positive signatures to five actual ANDs of free OR operands. It forces individual terminals to select one valid raw/helper or helper/helper pair. A Boolean flag distinguishes low or high in the fifth position when used as a raw partner. Its high case forbids truth on any selected small input and restricts the positive rows to one pencil. Its low case, and the fourth helper's low case, require the unique triple of the raw pair and forbid other selected inputs of size at most three. For a low fourth partner the common pencil point differs from the selected raw point, since that raw point is private and the helper is true on at least three lines. All tested inputs are drawn from the union of the retained five lines and have size at most four. The solver is allowed to use fewer rows than the entire downset. Refinement by a counterexample merely adds another necessary evaluation row. No inconclusive run is a proof.

The complete checked archive is small-core/five-partial-audit.json; naming a prefix specifies the CNF/LRAT stem under small-core/five-partial. Reproduce the generation by first running five-target-signatures.js. Then run five-partial.js K 8000 0 10000 --skip for K=1 to 5 in that order. Additional runs at different conflict budgets may provide other prefixes with identical semantics. The supplied checked archive includes five one-gate prefixes generated with budget 16000. Finally run audit-five-partial.js. It independently enumerates all 1048576 labeled candidate mask five-tuples on each representative, categorizes admissible terminal assignments by an alternative direct test and checks each admissible tuple is covered by a certified prefix after applying a verified stabilizer. It regenerates the DIMACS for the exact rows of every chosen certificate. The second RUP checker is ../plane920-search/check-rup.cjs. If the input already contains an empty clause it checks this directly. All 37808 admissible labeled five-tuples (10208, 6276, 21324 respectively) were covered by 354 prefixes, with 1303178 RUP additions verified independently of the initial stream-rup.cjs replays. The three orbits cover all 1287 choices of five complete lines.

## Every nine complete targets need seven helpers

If six helpers suffice for nine targets, each is active on at least five lines by the five-target result just proved. Every helper pair chosen for a terminal must consist of two signals, each active on exactly five of the nine rows. Their positive intersection is precisely the assigned row. Suppose two such edges share a center. Each of the two distinct neighbors must be absent on four of the center's five true lines and they therefore miss at least three rows together. This contradicts the joint-absence upper bound of two. The assigned edges form a matching on six helper vertices and there are at most three. Hence at least six targets have bare partners.

Every rank-four-or-earlier bare partner has at most four positive lines and is excluded. A bare partner in the fifth position must be low, also by the marked triangle certificate, and supplies at most one target. The last helper H therefore supplies at least five bare terminals; H is high. H cannot participate in a helper-pair terminal, since its partner would be false on all five or more of these marked lines and deleting it would leave a high with at most five ANDs accepting those five lines and at least one bare marker. This contradicts the pencil bound. The matching is on the other five helpers, so has size at most two. H must supply at least six of the required seven bare terminals. Then |R(H)|=6, all six lines have private points and no pair of earlier helpers can isolate an outside target by the two-pencil obstruction already verified. Nine targets exceed the supply of at most seven bare terminals. This proves the lower bound.

For the original seven-helper, thirteen-target problem, every helper now has at least five positive rows (nine-target/six-helper bound), every two jointly miss at most four rows (five-target/five-helper bound), every three jointly miss at most two, and every four jointly miss at most one. seven-edges-five.js 8 checks all 203490 labeled eight-edge graphs, all impossible. Thus at least six of the thirteen terminals need bare partners. The first four cannot provide any, and the fifth can provide only one, if low.
