# A unique OR does not save an AND

For distinct r-point lines intersecting pairwise in at most one point, computing the isolated line outputs on the downset of size r with a single dual OR still requires at least (r−1) times the number of lines **in AND gates alone**. Assume constants, repeated operands and identically equal full functions are bypassed. The preceding one-OR proof applies; this note supplies the strictness argument at equality.

Suppose there are just (r−1)n−1 ANDs for n outputs. The one-OR accounting shows smaller counts impossible. Its only possible collision is a single gate H AND p charged by two distinct lines L and M. The OR H has incomparable pure monomial branches A and B, of size at least two each; L chooses A and M chooses B. These are the only two lines needing different branches. Every AND must then be counted by some line and each line counts exactly r−1. Otherwise the upper bound of one double charge cannot supply all (r−1)n charges. The point p is the unique point in L intersect M.

In the circuit for L obtained by substituting the wire for A in place of H, inspect the full ancestor cone **before** bypassing any absorbed ANDs. Every AND in it is charged to L. Indeed a pure AND in the cone has a support of at least two (global normalization) inside L. It could not be counted by another line. For an original AND which used H, its pure base context is contained in L. A context of at least two points again prevents a charge to another line. An empty context was globally equal to H and was normalized away. For singleton context {q}, lines that set H true in the accounting see a single point and cannot count this gate. Lines choosing the same A would count a set of size at least two contained both in L and that other line, also impossible. The one remaining possibility is a charge to M using B, forcing q=p; every such gate is globally the identical function H AND p. Its unique normalized representative is already charged to L. This exhausts all ANDs because every AND must have some charge.

Thus the *full* selected cone itself contains exactly r−1 ANDs and all r sources of L. It is consequently an undirected tree, counting operand incidences as edges even if a substitution could have made two equal. The raw sources occur once in its unfolded proof. The output's pure base context C_L is therefore L minus A. If an A point occurred in the context as well as through the substituted H, there would be two source-to-output paths. In particular H occurs once in the selected proof. Likewise C_M=M minus B. The shared gate H AND p is counted in both cones, so p belongs to neither A nor B (otherwise that gate would be absorbed on that side). Hence B is disjoint from L and A from M.

Switching the OR branch in L now creates the valid sufficient input B union C_L. This is different from L, so must exceed the allowed size r. Its size is |B|+r−|A|, forcing |B|>|A|. Switching in M similarly forces |A|>|B|, a contradiction.

## Consequence at four ORs and 26 total gates

Use the three-elimination games and exceptional four-point-line classification in state/plane920-search/branch-game.md. If three OR eliminations preserve five outputs, their remaining subcircuit needs at least fifteen ANDs by the strict one-OR result (or by the pure bound if the OR disappears). The eight discarded sink terminals and all four ORs are separate additional gates, for a total of at least 27.

In the exceptional case the existing argument counts twenty-four external AND charges, the four ORs and the separate terminal of R, for twenty-nine before overlaps. At most two overlaps already give 27. If there are three overlaps in the singleton-versus-larger branch case, the singleton a must be enabled for its selected lines, and disabled on the colour of the line selecting the larger branch. Its fourth-OR operand thus actually conjoins external singleton a with a nonconstant R guard. The original generating AND is not one of the twenty-four charges because it has external support at most one for every fixed colour. This gives 29−3+1=27.

When the two operands have distinct singleton external supports a and b and both are used, each must be disabled in every colour where the opposite branch is selected by a line needing a branch; two distinct enabled singleton branches are incompatible with a genuinely needed branch. Both also are enabled somewhere. Thus each requires a guard AND, and the two original generating gates are distinct because their external supports differ. Neither gate is in the 24 charges, in the four ORs, or the sink terminal of R. At most four overlaps give 29−4+2=27. Other cases have at most two overlaps. Therefore four ORs are excluded in any 26-gate candidate, contingent on the finite three-elimination classification already checked by the companion work.
