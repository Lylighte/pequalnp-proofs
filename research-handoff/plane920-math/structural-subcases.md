# Two structural lower bounds for the order-three plane

These are partial results for L-meta-920. They do not assume formula topology. The final section records the missing unrestricted step.

Write (L_w=w+{0,1,3,9}) modulo 13. Every line has four points, and any two lines meet in a single point. In the dual circuit, inputs indicate the missing points (S); gates exchange AND and OR, and the output indexed by (w) must accept exactly (S=L_w) among sets of size at most four. A missing wire index in the original evaluates to zero, hence supplies a constant one in the dual. Propagating constants and unary operations, eliminating repeated operands, and redirecting equal functions to their first wire all weakly reduce size. Each operation preserves the dual circuit on the restricted domain. Ordinary nonconstant sources thereafter have one point each.

## A line with a four-leaf acceptance proof requires three private gates

On its own input line, unfold a true output into a proof tree by selecting one true child at each OR and both children at each AND. Its raw leaves use all four points, since their union is itself an accepted input. Call the tree clean if each point occurs just once. For any gate occurrence in the tree, let (T) be its descendant leaf labels. Its context outside this occurrence has leaf set (L_w\setminus T).

Take any minimum-cardinality input (B) making this gate true (on the restricted domain). Since (T) already makes it true, (|B|\le |T|). Substituting the new gate witness into the same proof makes the root true on (B\cup(L_w\setminus T)); this input has size at most four. It must be (L_w), so (T\subseteq B), whence (T=B). In particular *every* minimum-cardinality input of this gate is precisely (T).

Suppress unary tree nodes. Its four leaves have three binary branching occurrences. Their sets (T) have at least two points and are distinct: two branching occurrences either have disjoint supports, or one is a proper descendant with strictly smaller support. Thus they correspond to three different physical gates, regardless of global sharing. Their unique minimum input has at least two points on this line, so a branching gate charged in this fashion cannot be charged to another line. If (r) output lines admit clean trees, there are at least (3r) physical gates; if all thirteen do, there are at least 39.

The same substitution proves a useful unconditional inequality. In any (possibly unclean) tree occurrence, let (C) be its outside support and (d) the minimum true input size of its gate. Then (d\ge |L_w\setminus C|). Otherwise a minimum witness union (C) would have size less than four and would be accepted.

## The 39 bound with at most one OR in the dual

Pure AND circuits compute unions of the raw point labels of their predecessors, viewing a union as the set of required true inputs. A pure AND circuit whose output support is a four-point set (L) requires at least three binary merging gates of support size at least two contained in (L). To check this even with sharing, delete absorbed, constant and unary gates in the output cone. The four distinct sources must feed a connected cone of binary gates. A cone with (n) sources and (m) binary gates has at most (2m) operand edges but needs at least (n+m-1) edges, so (m\ge n-1). All surviving gates have support size at least two and are original gate identifiers. Supports of size at least two cannot lie in two different lines.

Suppose exactly one OR (H) remains in the dual. Its operands, computed before (H), are conjunctions with supports (A,B). A signal independent of (H) is a pure conjunction (C); every other signal is (H\land\bigwedge C), where (C) is obtained by treating (H) as the empty conjunction and stopping the backwards trace at (H). This is a syntactic occurrence flag, so redundant uses of (H) cause no problem. Pure outputs already have support (L).

For an output depending on (H) with context (C), correctness at (L) requires (C\subseteq L) and either (A\subseteq L) or (B\subseteq L). Also each of (A\cup C,B\cup C) must equal (L) or have size greater than four: each is a sufficient input. This criterion is both necessary and sufficient on the downset.

* If one operand is a constant, simplify the OR. If an operand's support exceeds four it is always false on this domain, so simplify. Nonconstant pure inputs with support at most four suffice from here.
* If (|A|=1), classify outputs into those with (C=L), plus all other outputs. Include pure outputs in the second class. For the first class fix (H:=1), obtaining the four-point conjunction (C). For the second class fix (H:=\bigwedge A) (a wire bypass); at an affected output (|C|\le3), so (|A\cup C|\le4) and (A\cup C=L). In either class the modified circuits use just the original AND gates, and each output requires three as above. Within one class they are disjoint. A gate charged to a first-class line has at least two raw context points: the bypass at (H:=1) does not traverse its operands. A second-class output using this gate must contain the same context points, whether or not this gate used (H). Thus cross-class charges are disjoint. This also covers two singleton operands by arbitrary choice of (A).
* Suppose both operands have size at least two. Any line whose output uses (H) contains (A) or (B), so at most two such lines exist. Choose for each an accepting operand (its union with the output context equals the line) and substitute this wire for (H). For pure outputs either substitution does. Each line again receives three original AND gates from its pure conjunction cone. Pure gates are private, because their support has size at least two. For a collision of a gate containing (H), the two lines must have chosen different operands. Write (p) for their unique common point. The gate's context is a subset of both lines, and so is empty or ({p}). With empty context its actual function is exactly (H) and it may be bypassed; a nonempty colliding gate's actual function is exactly (H\land p). All such gates are globally identical and can be redirected to the earliest one before counting. Hence the three charges per line can collide only once between a single pair of lines. The single OR (H) covers that possible deficit.

Consequently an original circuit with at most one AND (equivalently, at most one dual OR) needs at least 39 gates on the stated domain.

## Open case

The two arguments do not cover several dual OR gates and mandatory repeated-point proof trees. For example (((a\land b)\land(b\land c))\land(c\land d)) has no clean proof in its written form, despite being the four-point conjunction. Any full lower bound must account for gates that share across lines in different choices at multiple ORs, or prove a size-preserving simplification that removes them.
