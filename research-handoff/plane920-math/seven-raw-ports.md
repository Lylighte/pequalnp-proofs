# With seven hidden ANDs the full thirteen-line problem needs at least twelve ORs

This is a gate-count statement in the sparse dual. All thirteen targets equal one on their own line and zero on every other input of size at most four. For inputs these are ordinary raw Boolean variables. Count binary ORs, binary hidden ANDs, and the thirteen distinct terminal ANDs separately.

## Relocating the terminal ANDs preserves the OR budget

First fold constants, copies, and duplicate operands. If an output wire is an OR that computes one of the targets, descend through any child that is true on its unique positive row L. The child must be false on every promised negative row of that target. Continue until an AND is reached. A raw point cannot compute the target. Let T_L be the selected AND. The thirteen selected ANDs are distinct, since their restrictions to the promised domain are different.

Separate the thirteen gates T_L from the remainder of the network. In the remaining physical gate list substitute zero for every reference to a selected terminal. Form the new terminal for L from the two old input wires of T_L, making the same substitution if one of them itself names a selected terminal. The new terminals are sinks. On L, every selected terminal other than T_L was zero in the old circuit, so evaluating the ancestors of T_L after the substitution leaves its positive value unchanged. Its own value cannot occur among those ancestors by topological order. On all other promised inputs the new values are at most the old ones by monotonicity. One can fold the newly introduced zero wires. This operation reuses each terminal AND, adds no OR gates, and does not add any hidden AND gates. The surviving nonterminal physical gates remain in their old dependency order.

For a fixed L, both input arms of its new terminal are true on L. Follow a true child through OR gates in each arm until reaching a raw point or a surviving nonterminal AND output. These are its two chosen atoms. Each is a pointwise lower bound on the value of the initial arm, and is true on L. Consequently rewiring this terminal directly to those two already available wires preserves all promised outputs. Choices are independent across terminals because they are now sinks. This rewiring bypasses OR gates on the *terminal's input edges only*. A bypassed OR that also feeds the hidden network stays there with exactly its old input edges. No copy of an OR gate and no new implementation of a raw point is needed. If the selected pair repeats the same hidden atom g, replace one copy by any p in L. The new terminal is g AND p. It still accepts L, and is bounded by the previous repeated-atom terminal g. Two raw atoms cannot form a terminal for a four-point target. Unused physical gates may now be discarded. The retained hidden subcircuit consists of the AND ancestors of the atoms used at terminals and their original intervening OR gates. Its OR count h is certainly at most the original OR count.

## Each hidden AND is true on five or more target lines

By partial-helper-proof.md, no six hidden ANDs with free OR can realize nine complete plane targets. Suppose there are seven hidden ANDs for the full thirteen. If one g is false on nine target rows, substituting false for g preserves the positive value on all nine and decreases other values. This gives the forbidden six-helper circuit for those nine targets. Every hidden AND is therefore true on at least five targets. In particular it has no direct physical raw-point input: the four lines through a given point are all the positive lines on which that input is true. This inference concerns the circuit as normalized above, after any rewiring and deletion. If that process removed a hidden AND entirely, the full set would already be impossible with six.

A low helper accepts some input of size at most three. A terminal p AND g for L with a low g forces its only accepted input of size at most three to be T=L minus p: for any such U, the promised input U union {p} must be L. No low helper can supply bare terminals on two lines, since distinct lines share only one point. Under the rank-four local certificates in partial-helper-proof.md any of the first four helpers, low or high, can be a bare partner only if it is true on at most four full target lines. This conflicts with the five-line activation lower bound. Thus the number of distinct low bare partners, and hence the number of corresponding bare targets in this circuit, is at most three. No condition on the total number of high helpers is needed.

## Counting physical raw ports

A raw point that enters the retained hidden network must do so through an OR port, since a physical hidden AND cannot directly read it. If p is never read by a hidden OR gate, none of the hidden atoms depends on it. For each of the four target lines L through p, switching the input from L to the triple L minus p leaves all hidden atoms and all other raw atoms unchanged. The terminal's fixed atom pair must directly read p to distinguish the two inputs. Its other atom is a hidden helper that is true on the triple as well as the four-set. This gives four different low bare targets, impossible by the preceding paragraph. Thus every one of the thirteen raw points occurs in some input port of a retained hidden OR.

Count the t raw input ports across the h surviving physical OR gates. The count includes repeated occurrences of the same variable at different gates. A variable with just one occurrence is called singly read here. If s variables are singly read, then t is at least s+2(13-s), giving s at least 26-t. At most 2h-t of these singly-read variables can have their sole occurrence in an OR combining the variable with a nonraw input: each such gate uses a distinct nonraw OR port. All other singly-read variables occur in a binary raw OR p OR q with q distinct from p (a same-variable OR was folded). Hence at least 26-2h singly-read variables occur in pure raw ORs. Unary ORs or constants have already been folded; an upper bound of 2h ports suffices in any case.

For such a p use the unique projective line L containing p and its partner q. The input to the hidden network via p OR q is true on both L and L minus p because q remains present. This is p's only physical input port in the hidden network, so every hidden signal has exactly the same value on these two rows. The terminal for L must explicitly read p. Its other atom, necessarily hidden, is true on the triple L minus p, so it is a low bare partner. If different singly-read variables forced the same line's terminal to read both raw variables explicitly, the pair of raw atoms would accept a two-point input. Therefore the forced target lines are distinct. The low helpers used for them are likewise distinct by uniqueness of T. Their number is at most three.

It follows that 26-2h is at most three, hence h is at least twelve. In particular a seven-helper circuit with nine physical OR gates is impossible. This result uses the independently replayed five-target lower bound through the nine-target deduction and the small rank-four bare-partner certificates. It does not need a classification of the seven-helper endpoint graph or the complete six-auxiliary, thirteen-line classification.
