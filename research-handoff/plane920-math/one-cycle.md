# Connected 13-source hidden graphs with one cycle

Write "merge" for a gate whose two operands belonged to distinct hidden components immediately before adding the gate in the chosen topological order. A gate whose two operands were already connected is a surplus gate. A connected graph with thirteen raw sources and thirteen nonconstant binary hidden gates has exactly one surplus gate. The output terminals are kept as separate sinks.

## Any surplus OR keeps the eight-AND cut recurrence

More generally, suppose every surplus gate is an OR. In a fixed consistent proof of a positive input, discard the unused edge of each surplus OR. For any surplus OR not in the proof, discard an arbitrary one of its two inputs as well. The hidden graph so obtained is a forest. Inductively, each merger still joins two distinct components, and each former surplus gate attaches as a new vertex on one edge. The partition of previously existing vertices into components is unchanged after each step: the two original operands at a surplus gate were already connected. Thus every accepting proof of a hidden wire has an underlying tree and has no repeated raw source. In particular a wire with unique global minimum witness X still has |X|−1 genuine AND gates carrying unique minimum supports of size at least two inside X. A minimum-support substitution here is in the original circuit; no global choice of OR edges across different lines is necessary.

Use the same hierarchy of merge times as in state/plane920-core/connected-tree.md. At each component U take the moment just after its forming merge H and before later gates. Previous surplus ORs lie entirely on one side of this merge, with their original functions. Freeze outside U at the characteristic values of the target L. For this one line bypass each *later* surplus OR to a child that preserves its value at L. Select in topological order at the original row, which keeps every wire's value on that row. On all inputs each bypass weakly decreases the circuit. All later true merges have at most one child depending on U, because only one of their pre-existing components contains U. After freezing they become constants or transmit one designated wire from U. In the frozen modified circuit, the terminal for L is therefore the conjunction of at most two original wires from the specified boundary of U. It is true on S=L intersect U and false on every different U subset of size at most |S|, by monotone decrease from the original correct circuit. This is exactly the property used in the local cut argument. The drivers of H are not bypassed here because only surplus ORs after H were rewritten; their unique-minimum labels are fixed across all L. Their own acceptance proofs are trees by the previous paragraph.

Consequently the existing 8,191-subset cut recurrence and its lower bound of eight genuine hidden AND gates still apply without modification when all surplus gates are ORs. With 26 total gates, 13 terminal ANDs and 13 hidden gates, there can then be at most five ORs. The existing separate h≤3 exclusion at that budget leaves four or five; the four-OR exclusion for 25 gates alone does not remove four at 26.

As a numerical control, cut-budget.js gives a more permissive one-surplus-OR DP that separates AND lower bounds by side and considers the disjoint-positive-set OR cost. It allows a crossing slice to waive both costs in the one overlapping class; this is a relaxation. It returns eight for both a tree and a tree with an extra OR. The control strengthens some local constraints but does not raise the bound.

For each side A of a split, put n_A equal to the sum of |L intersect A|−1 over the crossing lines. When the last merge H is AND, all waived positive costs on A must have the same opposite slice y. If j lines use that waiver and j>1, their common y is a singleton, so their A-slices are nonempty and pairwise disjoint. The A driver of H is true on all these slices, requiring at least j−1 OR gates in A (replacing every AND by one child gives a larger pure OR still hitting every disjoint slice). For each y and j the program tries the sum w of its largest j available costs. The A-side AND bound is max(D(A), n_A−w); it must fit within |A|−1 actual mergers even when the spare gate is an OR, and together with j−1 ORs it must fit within |A|−1+e_A total gates. The same is tried independently on B, allowing incompatible simultaneous waivers as a relaxation. The resource e_A+e_B≤1 is carried through the recursion; assigning the possible extra OR to a child more liberally than actual chronology is safe. An OR final merge has zero exemptions and is checked against the same component capacities.

At the full root the final hidden gate must be AND in an unrefutable 26-gate candidate, since a last OR could always be removed as above, giving at most 25 gates. The spare OR must have occurred before this last AND. With eight hidden ANDs the root-split histogram in cut-budget.json leaves only the 13 singleton cuts and 78 two-point cuts. Each of the two possible locations of the spare OR is counted in the JSON; the singleton component itself cannot contain an additional normalized binary gate. No split with smaller side size at least three survives the relaxation.

## Reordering the unique cycle

Give the edges of the unique undirected cycle their circuit orientations. Any local sink on the cycle (both incident cycle edges enter it) can be moved after every other cycle vertex in some topological order. It has no directed path to another cycle vertex: such a path would have to leave the cycle and return at a distinct vertex, producing a second undirected cycle. In the new order this sink is the surplus gate. Thus any OR which is a local cycle sink puts the graph in the preceding case.

If the oriented cycle has at least two local sinks, no individual hidden wire has an entire cycle in its syntactic ancestor graph. Otherwise every vertex of the cycle would have a directed path to that wire. The outside of a unicyclic connected graph consists of trees, each attached at just one cycle vertex. No two distinct local sinks can reach the same vertex. In this case all hidden wires are read-once formulas even when every cycle sink is an AND. The two-wire projection step at an intermediate cut is still subject to the exception below.

## A fixed pair for the remaining surplus AND

Consider a component U already formed before the surplus AND E. Before E, a wire enclosing U depends after outside freezing on at most one *structurally designated* wire at the U boundary: its other predecessor component is disjoint from U, so after freezing the gate either transmits the designated wire or becomes constant. The identity of this boundary wire does not vary with the outside values whenever dependence remains. Thus the two E operands can project only to two fixed U wires r and s or constants. E projects to a constant, to r or s, or to r AND s. All gates after E are ordinary merges, so after freezing they again transmit at most one of the boundary signals or this E expression. Each target section is consequently isolated either by the usual AND of at most two U wires or by (r AND s) AND h for one U wire h. The fixed pair is the same for every target. This identifies the single departure from the ordinary local demand whenever a surplus AND is later than the formation of U. No bound of 27 is inferred here.
