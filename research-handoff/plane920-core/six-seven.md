# The 26-gate OR counts

In the sparse dual variables, thirteen AND terminals leave 13 hidden gates. Write h for the number of dual ORs. Before this step h at most five and every disconnected case had been excluded by separate arguments.

## Eight or more ORs

After permitting arbitrary free ORs, each terminal can be narrowed to the AND of two atoms true on its own positive line. An atom is a raw variable or the output of a hidden AND. Shrinking its inputs only decreases the output and preserves that line. Any three distinct full plane targets need at least five hidden ANDs in this relaxed model. The concurrent and nonconcurrent cases have independently replayed RUP certificates in ../plane920-math/small-core/deleted/family-35-4-s-125000.json and family-7-4-s-125000.json. These subfamilies transfer by freezing unused coordinates to zero. This immediately rules out four or fewer hidden ANDs.

Suppose there are five hidden ANDs. Every helper is true on at least eleven of the thirteen positive lines. If it were false on three, fixing it to false would give those three targets with only four helpers. Two helper atoms therefore are true together on at least nine lines. A helper and a raw point share at least two of its four lines. Neither terminal pair isolates its positive row. Two raw inputs accept an input of size at most two. This excludes five helpers as well, hence h at least eight.

## Six ORs

For any five distinct retained targets, a circuit with at most two ORs needs at least eight hidden ANDs, in addition to its terminals. With at most one OR, use the strict one-OR bound in ../plane920-math/strict-one-or.md. For two ORs, if an operand forces two distinct raw points then bypass its OR to the opposite operand. At most one retained target is lost. The remaining at least four targets need at least twelve ANDs with at most one OR, hence eight hidden ANDs. In the other case the first OR joins two distinct raw points. On the full cube each second OR operand is a conjunction of at most one raw point and possibly the first OR. After geometric normalization, the 550 programs in ../plane920-search/two-or-private.cjs cover all possibilities after removing redundant operations. The five smallest per-line private lower bounds sum to at least seven. Even when just five rows are specified as positive targets, using strongest common factors from every other named plane line remains a relaxation. If a non-private AND is present, this gives at least eight hidden ANDs. If all hidden ANDs are private, each of the five targets needs two true hidden ANDs in its accepting proof to combine its four distinct raw sources in addition to its terminal. This requires ten in total.

It follows that resolving the first four of six ORs globally to chosen operands can never retain five outputs in a seven-hidden-AND counterexample. The complete four-prefix archive and forest reduction from five-private.md therefore apply unchanged. All 167,922 five-OR extension functions have combined private weight at least twenty. The row-wise tests agree between full-private.cjs on 715 rows and ../plane920-search/private-independent.cjs on 1,093 rows. Their identical histograms appear in five-weighted.json and private-independent-full.json. Bypass the final OR to either operand in two alternative circuits. Every positive row survives in at least one of them. One alternative keeps row weights totaling at least ten, whereas there are only seven hidden ANDs. These weights are valid for any retained subset because the free library for each row already allowed sharing with all thirteen possible target rows. Thus the six-OR case is excluded.

## Seven ORs

With five retained lines and at most three ORs, six hidden ANDs cannot suffice. To handle an OR arm forcing two raw points, take the first such arm in topological order. Its producer is a hidden AND, since an earlier OR that forced both points would already have an arm forcing both points. Replacing the OR by its other arm discards at most one target line. Discard the line containing that pair if present. On the remaining positive lines this supplier AND is false and may also be set to false. At five lines this gives at least four targets, at most two ORs, and at most five hidden ANDs. The two-OR four-line bound used here holds even at six hidden ANDs. Its own multi-point case reduces to three targets, at most one OR and at most five hidden ANDs, contradicting the strict one-OR bound.

For the cases without such an arm, enumerate the small programs exactly as in ../plane920-search/three-or-five-lines.cjs. The projective collineation group is transitive on unordered pairs; fixing the first pair leaves a stabilizer of size 72. There are 19 two-OR orbits and 4,483 three-OR orbits. For each line first grant all shared terms for free as above. If the resulting private sum for the five cheapest targets is smaller than six, enumerate the additionally affordable shared terms. Every actual shared AND, stopped at raw inputs and named ORs, is their conjunction. If active on multiple target rows it has at most one raw factor. Group such expressions by the complete set of named positive rows where they activate, and replace each one with the conjunction of its entire equivalence class. This preserves its positives and strengthens the term. Shared gates that coincide with an already free atom cost a physical gate but add no extra term. Keeping the distinct new terms, and charging at least one if any real shared gate was required, is a relaxation. Recompute private requirements for each possible shared library. With no shared gates a family of five requires ten private hidden gates directly. This excludes the 140 three-OR orbits and the one two-OR orbit that needed this refinement. See few-or-3-5-6.json and few-or-2-4-6.json. Thus any four early resolutions out of seven may retain at most four lines, and the same critical four-prefix archive applies.

For each of the 167,922 five-OR extensions, take the same per-line private costs. After a global resolution of any later OR, one can type its arm on every positive row as an intersection of a subset of the five fixed OR signatures with the positive-row signature of a raw monomial. The latter is empty, full, a singleton line, or a four-line pencil. The possible values form an overapproximation even after resolving another late OR, since its selected arm contributes just another conjunction. At each resolution of arms A,B the row set S splits into two overlapping alternatives S minus (A minus B) and S minus (B minus A). two-late.cjs gives the second OR independently to the adversary for each first history. To leave weight at most six on all four histories, each first history must have weight at most twelve since its two second outcomes cover it. Test the exact exclusive weights at both choices. This relaxation rules out all but 48 of the five-OR extensions.

For each of the 48, enumerate the actual sixth OR using the same genuine component-merge condition and normalized full truth functions. There are 46,956 extensions, with private weights respectively 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 in counts 72, 24, 696, 5,916, 12,690, 8,250, 19,308. Selecting the better child of the final seventh OR therefore retains weight at least nine for the fixed first six, above the budget of six. Reproduce from or-forest.cjs with --five --private --full --private-only --weighted --two-late followed by --five --private --full --close-late. See two-late-results.json and sixth-late-results.json.
