# Excluding a circuit of size 27

Normalize the heavy-input circuit in its sparse dual on at most four missing points. Take a minimum-size circuit with a minimum number of dual OR gates. There are thirteen disjoint terminal AND gates. At size 27, h dual OR gates leave 14−h hidden AND gates. The previous unconditional bound of 27 is in six-seven.md. This calculation uses no assumption about the number of cycles in the hidden graph.

For at most three ORs, the earlier elimination bound gives size at least 29. For four ORs, unless the first three are the exceptional raw OR tree on a line, some choice of three global bypasses retains six targets. The remaining single-OR subprogram needs at least twelve hidden ANDs for those six targets, exceeding the budget of ten. For the exceptional case, four-tail.cjs regenerates all 234 topological mergers of four points on a line. For each fixed triple it allows any later conjunction arm on the named positives and both choices of the last OR. No pattern fits the budget.

For five through seven ORs, retaining five targets after resolving the first four is impossible. With five ORs the remaining one-OR circuit would need ten hidden ANDs and has nine. The corresponding two- and three-OR subfamily lower bounds were checked in ../plane920-search/five-lines-27.md. The four-prefix classification from ../plane920-search/five-or-prefix.md is therefore available. Use all 1,217 representatives. No prefix is excluded for overlapping supports.

At each fixed prefix, the universe of positive-row signatures of later resolved operands is covered by all intersections of the fixed OR signatures with signatures of arbitrary raw monomials. As in six-seven.md, enumerate orientation choices and retain only rows on which each chosen arm can preserve the OR's value. To relax the real circuit, let later arm pairs vary with previously selected arms. A gate with one arm dominated by the opposite arm on all named positives could be bypassed globally in the original minimum circuit. For each distinct fixed operand that is neither raw nor a previously existing OR, also charge its required physical AND gate. If true on precisely one of the thirteen named lines it contributes to that line's private floor. Otherwise add it to the separate fixed cost. Using the maximum between its private requirement and the number of fixed gates private to the same line avoids charging one gate twice.

For a retained set of lines S, a private cost at a line is computed with the strongest free expressions shared with *any* other named line, even one outside S. Thus each charged gate can be true on just its own named line in the modified circuit. Apart from the direct sum test, choose one, two or three lines of S. The other charged private gates are all false on these chosen positives and may be removed simultaneously. The chosen family still needs at least 2, 4 or 5 hidden ANDs respectively. In this additional test fixed shared gates are allowed to contribute in full to the small subproblem. The tests are monotone upon shrinking the set of retained lines. When all possible continuations of an alleged adversarial split include a retained set that violates a lower bound, discard the prefix. The code is late-game.cjs and unrestricted-prefix.cjs.

At four fixed ORs the relaxed game and the positive dominance test leave 0, 168 and 451 prefixes for h=5,6,7. The three full shard logs collectively cover ids 0 to 1216 with no duplicates. All 1,918,815 fifth operand pairs actually expanded across the combined h=5,6,7,8 run satisfy the prefix normalizations. After this stage there are no unresolved h=5 or h=6 programs. For h=7, 28 fifth-OR programs remain. tail-refine.cjs enumerates all 170,632 normalized sixth operand pairs in these programs. The one remaining OR fails the retained-cost test in every case. Checks and aggregate results are under unrestricted.

For h at least eight there are at most six hidden ANDs. There is a stronger OR-independent exclusion in the free-OR relaxation. Stop a terminal AND at raw variables and previous helper ANDs, selecting at each input a single true atom on the required positive line. Thirteen distinct targets require more than six such helpers. The elimination and independent receipts are under ../plane920-math and unrestricted/two-high. Significant dependencies are the three-line and two-line helper lower bounds, the four-small-helper covering test, the six-vertex terminal pairing graph calculation, and the geometric classification of positive line sets with many private points. In particular, the four-edge graph accepted by the pairing calculation is a four-leaf star. Only the verified upper bound of four edges is used for the geometric classification.

For the six-helper calculation, call a helper high if it is false on all sets of size at most three. Each of the other, low, helpers can be partnered with a raw point on at most one target, because its small activating set must be the target triple obtained by removing that point. The first helper cannot be so partnered. The helper lower bounds imply that each pair of helpers can both be false on at most two positive lines. The incidence/terminal pairing reductions force two high helpers in the last two positions and at most six unordered orbits for their positive signatures. In each orbit, if two helper atoms form a terminal they must be exactly the two high helpers: each high is inactive on at least six lines, and every other pair is simultaneously active on at least two of these. A terminal using the same helper twice can instead be paired with a raw point of its required line.

two-high-cnf.cjs encodes unrestricted disjunctions on both operands of each of the six hidden ANDs. All thirteen positive rows and their 52 one-point deletions suffice. Both orders of all six high-signature pairs are impossible. audit-two-high.cjs independently rebuilds every CNF and verifies its positive-hint proof to the empty clause using a second RUP checker, 9,239 total additions. The positive control high-controls.cjs control constructs two isolated full-line outputs with six helpers, pins their selectors, satisfies the same encoder restricted to those two targets, and checks their truth values on all 1,093 permissible inputs. Three unstrengthened exploratory runs with a different encoder timed out and were not used. There is an alternative short proof using the local four-AND witness tests instead of the twelve signature-specific CNFs.

Together these cases establish the mathematical and finite-computational lower bound 28. The unrestricted machine-checked Lean result remains 25. The project asks for 39.
