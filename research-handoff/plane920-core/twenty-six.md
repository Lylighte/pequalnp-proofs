# Consequences for a circuit with 26 gates

Work in the dual on at most four zero points. The construction of disjoint terminal output gates leaves at most thirteen hidden gates. The refined four-OR argument in ../plane920-search/branch-game.md gives at least five dual ORs for any circuit of size at most 26. Therefore there are at most eight hidden dual ANDs. A crossing line in a disconnected hidden graph forces a separate wire of uniquely minimum activating support equal to each nonempty slice in its component. On inputs in that component of weight at most the size of the slice its signal agrees with the conjunction testing that slice. Any slice of size at least two has a representative AND: take an earliest wire with that restricted function; an OR would have an active child on the slice, and the child could not be active on any other permitted input for the restricted function. Slices of size at least two on different lines do not yield the same representative (a smaller minimum, or a different minimum of the same size, would violate one of the two restricted specifications).

The classification in hidden-components.json still restricts a disconnected graph to two components with a smaller block of at most three points: all excluded partitions require at least fourteen hidden gates. If the smaller block has three points, at least nine crossing lines have slices of size two or three in the larger block. Collinear triples produce nine three-point slices; noncollinear triples give six triples and three pairs. These require nine distinct hidden ANDs and are impossible.

For a smaller block of two points, their joining line contributes one pair in each block. Six other crossing lines contribute six different triples in the larger block. Thus all eight hidden ANDs are accounted for. Each of the six triples is disjoint from the larger block's distinguished pair, since the joining line and a singleton-crossing line meet at one of the two small points. Choose the earliest of the six triple representative ANDs. If the pair representative occurs before it, then it is the first AND in the larger block. It is a conjunction of two disjunctions of raw inputs, whose every activating input contains an activating subset of size at most two. Since the pair is its unique admissible activating input on at most two points, its full function is exactly that pair. It is false on the chosen triple. Thus any accepting parse of the earliest triple can follow its two operands through ORs to at most two true raw points. This contradicts the requirement that the triple have smallest activating size three. Choosing the earliest triple matters because the pair obligation by itself has no semantic requirement on three-point inputs. SmallCuts.lean verifies the normalized geometric counts and disjointness, fixing a small point at zero; translating the point and line indices gives the other cases.

For a single isolated point, the large component must already compute four disjoint triple indicators on the domain of size at most three. The previous free-OR lower bound requires eight ANDs exactly. Every one of these hidden ANDs must participate in at least one of the four fixed accepting proofs; otherwise it could be set false, leaving all four positive rows unchanged and possibly only decreasing values at negative rows. Nine other lines are four-point transversals of the triple partition. On each such transversal the earliest active hidden AND must have a certificate of size at most two supported on different triple groups. To see that some hidden AND is needed, its terminal AND and pure OR ancestors alone would require at most two raw points. A triple representative cannot be that earliest AND, since it has no activation on up to two points. This gives a testable extra condition for the remaining singleton case; a full feasibility check is still open here.

For connected 26-gate graphs, the hidden graph has one independent undirected cycle after simplification when all thirteen hidden gates are present. If the redundant merge in a topological ordering is an OR, choosing an active child separately for a fixed proof removes its cycle. The preceding hidden-tree slice lower bound on eight dual ANDs survives; this case can now occur only with exactly five dual ORs and eight hidden ANDs. If the redundant merge is an AND, the two values it merges may give an extra conjunct across a cut. The ordinary tree recurrence cannot be applied directly.

hard-budget.cjs and hard-driver-budget.cjs implement relaxations of the ordinary tree recurrence with additional per-slice uniqueness constraints. The latter includes a lower bound on ORs needed by a common driver to activate on disjoint input sets and a possible extra OR inside a child. These relaxed conditions still admit eight-AND abstract patterns. They do not establish any circuit existence or solve the 26-gate case.

## The isolated-input case is now excluded

For four disjoint triples on at most three active points, eight dual ANDs are necessary even if ORs are free. If only eight are available, take the four earliest triple representatives and isolate them on this restricted domain. They can be made distinct terminal ANDs; their erased uses are always false on at most two points, so this transformation preserves every other wire on those rows. Flatten the OR paths. At each terminal keep one atom on each side that is active on its required positive triple. Shrinking these inputs preserves the desired output at the positive row and cannot introduce a false positive. The other four ANDs are helpers. Each terminal now reads a within-group raw point and a helper or two distinct helpers. A helper replacing both sides would already give the same target and contradict the seven-AND lower bound after that terminal is removed.

The independent finite check in ../plane920-search/disjoint-four-cross.md proves that no helper in such a normalized four-triple model can activate on any cross-group pair. All 400 symmetry-reduced patterns are covered, with one pattern divided into nine cases, yielding 408 cases. One CNF has the empty clause outright. The other 407 have independently replayed clause proofs. The root-side cross-receipt-audit.cjs also decompresses the stored proofs and verifies each length, digest and total independently from the search program. The 93,307,145 stored bytes account for 1,095,002 added clauses. A transversal target would require just such a helper: the first hidden AND active on a transversal can be made active by at most two raw points in different groups, since prior active wires are ORs of true inputs. A representative of a triple cannot activate on such a set. If the needed witness has fewer than two points, extend it inside the transversal to two using monotonicity. If no hidden AND activates on the transversal, its terminal alone likewise cannot require four raw points. This excludes the singleton disconnected case and, with the earlier two- and three-point arguments, every disconnected 26-gate candidate.

## Connected single-cycle case

When an ordering makes the redundant merge an OR, the last splitting case 2+11 from ../plane920-math/cut-budget.json is also excluded. The budget forces seven hidden ANDs in the eleven-point component. Six different ANDs have unique minimum supports of size at least two inside three disjoint triples, two per triple. The seventh has the complementary two-point slice as its unique minimum. These four blocks partition all eleven points. Pick one of the full four-point lines meeting each block once. The earliest large-component hidden AND active there would have an activating subset of at most two transversal points, since earlier active operations can be traced back through ORs. None of the seven required labels can be contained in this subset. No hidden AND in that component can help; the small-component signals and the final AND joining the components are false on this line. This contradicts a four-point target. The detailed split count is in ../plane920-math/one-cycle.md.

For the remaining redundant-OR case, the final merge isolates one point p. The mixed signal has form p AND f, where f is a signal on the other twelve points. Some lines through p can read this mixed signal, so the disconnected-case certificate cannot be reused verbatim here. At least two of the four disjoint triples must use f as a common factor in a terminal conjunction. SharedSlice.lean proves the following general local rule: whenever an output isolates a set T and is the AND of increasing signals, any two activating sets whose sizes sum to at most |T| must be complementary subsets of T and individually unique minima. In particular, if f has a singleton witness outside T, its partner for a triple T must have minimum activating size at least three. This local result has been independently replayed. A circuit with a redundant AND is a different open case. The unrestricted bound remains 26.
