# A 25-gate lower bound for arbitrary AND/OR circuits in L-meta-920

This argument uses the order-three incidence structure and the heavy-input correctness condition. All gate operations below are in the dual on sets of at most four zero inputs. A line output accepts exactly its own four-point row. Normalize constants, unary copies and globally duplicated wires. Choose each designated line output as the earliest gate computing its restricted function. It must be a genuine dual AND: a dual OR active on its line has a child active on that line, and neither child can be active on any other admissible input. The number of gates does not increase during normalization. Below m is the number of remaining gates and h is the number of dual ORs.

For each line choose once and for all one consistent accepting sub-DAG on its row. It takes one child at a dual OR, both at a dual AND and contains the four points of the line. It contains no designated output of a different line. Use the clean/anchored decomposition of ../plane920-math/cycle-anchor.md. To recap, cut the ancestor part of the latest vertex on a cycle. Any bypass of the cut would give a cycle ending later, so its point support T is disjoint from the rest of the accepting proof. Replacing it by a minimum accepting set forces that set to equal T. If T is a singleton, retain an original directed path and treat it as a leaf whose value that point forces. If T has two or more points, its gate uniquely determines the line and supplies an anchor cycle. This produces b anchored lines. Each of the remaining 13-b lines has a clean proof with three different binary dual ANDs whose unique minimum sets determine that line. The b anchored lines still require b distinct AND outputs. Thus
     
    m ≥ 39 − 2b + h.

The previously proven one-OR bound in ../plane920-search/one-and-bound.md implies h≥2 in any supposed circuit smaller than 39.

Fix a pencil of four lines through a point p. Its chosen proof union is connected and contains 13 raw points. At least the other nine designated outputs are absent. Let v be the gates in this union, r its cycle rank and d its sum of gate indegree deficits relative to two. Thus r=v−12−d. Each absent nondesignated gate consumes one more unit of m−9−v. The anchor cycles of the anchored lines in this pencil are independent, since each has a distinct topologically greatest vertex. Write b_p for the number of anchored lines in this pencil. If a physical OR is used with fewer than two different operand choices across these four proofs, either it is absent or its indegree is one. Denote by e_p the number of the first two OR gates that fail to use both sides in this pencil. Both are nondesignated. These facts give

    b_p + e_p ≤ m−21.

Sum over the thirteen pencils. Every line occurs four times, so the left side sums to 4b plus the sum of e_p. The first two ORs supply a total deficit of at least four:

* Both inputs to the first OR are nonconstant pure monomials and each requires some raw point, say x and y (possibly the same). Let R,B be the line proofs choosing its respective sides. The choices are disjoint because the selection per line is fixed. R is contained in the pencil through x, and B in the pencil through y. The two complete pencils share a line. Thus at least one side misses a line in its own pencil. Its at most three concurrent lines together cover at most ten points. On at least three of the thirteen pencils this side is never used.
* Let H be the first OR. Before the second OR every wire is a nonempty pure monomial, H, or H AND a nonempty pure monomial. This follows inductively by taking unions of point supports and remembering whether H is present. If neither child of the second OR required any raw point, both would equal H and this OR would already have been removed. Some side therefore requires a point z. If the proofs selecting that side omit one of the four lines through z, this side misses at least three pencils. If they include all four, none of those four can select the other side, which is then absent at the pencil through z. In either case the second OR contributes at least one.

Hence 4b+4 ≤13(m−21). For m≤24 this forces b≤8. But m≥39−2b+h and h≥2 imply b≥9. We conclude that every circuit meeting the posted assumptions has **at least 25 gates**.

Independent review checked the disjoint choices and the simultaneous accounting of absent and unary gates. BranchDeficit.lean proves the first-OR and second-OR incidence bounds and the final arithmetic implication. The decomposition and its link to arbitrary project circuits are mathematical proofs; the largest universal lower bound currently linked end-to-end to the project's circuit type in Lean is 21. The posted bound is 39.

The executable anchor-deficit.cjs also explores a relaxation that tests the corresponding inequalities on every subfamily of lines, with first-OR point pair fixed to 0 and 1. For 24 gates it finds no eligible anchor pattern. For 25 gates it leaves 1,450 patterns in this relaxation. These are combinatorial possibilities, not candidate circuits.
