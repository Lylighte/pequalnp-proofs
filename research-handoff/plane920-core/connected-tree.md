# Excluding a connected hidden tree with seven hidden dual ANDs

Suppose the hidden graph is a tree on thirteen raw inputs and twelve hidden gates, with thirteen additional terminal dual ANDs. Remove any unary or constant wires first. Each hidden gate joins two previously disconnected components. Indeed joining two wires in an already connected component would create an undirected cycle. The last gate in a component U therefore joins disjoint blocks A,B. Apart from this final hidden gate H, every wire in U belongs to only one block. This gives a recursive partition of the raw points by merge time.

For every component U in this partition and each original line L, freeze all raw points outside U to their characteristic values on L. Each hidden wire from a subsequent merge becomes constant or a projection of one wire in U. To check this assertion, start when U is formed and work forwards: any later merge has at most one child from the component containing U, the other child being independent of U. The terminal computing L therefore restricts to the conjunction of at most two U wires, allowing identities and constants. On all U-sets of size at most |S| where S=L∩U, this conjunction is active exactly at S. The rows used here are allowed because the frozen points contribute 4−|S|. This target-specific freezing preserves the actual internal wires; each line can be checked with its own frozen input.

Consider S crossing the final cut A,B of U. Write x=S∩A and y=S∩B. In the ordinary case, one pure wire in each block has unique minimum activating set x or y, respectively. Specifically, if different pure wires feed the terminal, fixing the opposite block at its slice establishes this claim; no smaller or different equal-size witness survives. If a mixed H is the only effective terminal operand, it must be an AND of the two pure drivers and both have the stated property. If one operand is H and the other is a pure A wire, fixing A at x shows that the B driver of H has unique minimum y. If H is an OR, its A driver is false at x (otherwise x alone activates the terminal), and fixing B to y now sets H true. Thus the separate A operand has unique minimum x. The symmetric case is identical. If H is an AND, the separate A-side minimum x need not occur on a single wire. The only exception waiving x is therefore when the fixed B driver of H has unique minimum y. Waiving y requires the fixed A driver to have unique minimum x. At most one side can be waived on each line. A pure operation on only a single block cannot realize a crossing S.

A signal of the hidden tree with unique minimum activating set x supplies |x|−1 distinct dual ANDs with distinct unique-minimum labels of size at least two, contained in x. To see this, take a consistent acceptance tree on x. The underlying hidden graph is acyclic, so its active leaves do not repeat. Their set must be x. At a binary branching vertex replace its subproof by any minimum activating set for that vertex. The replacement and the disjoint remaining leaves activate the parent signal with size at most |x|, hence their union is x. This forces the replacement to equal that subtree's point set. The branching vertices are dual ANDs. Such charges on different original lines cannot coincide since any two of those lines share at most one point.

Let D(U) be the computed lower bound on hidden dual ANDs. For a split, the child-count lower bound is d=D(A)+D(B). For its crossing slices, put c=sum(|x|−1+|y|−1). If H is OR, the split lower bound is max(d,c). If H is AND, try all possible fixed driver minima P,Q from the A- and B-slices (plus a nonmatching sentinel). Each crossing slice may save at most the larger of |x|−1 if y=Q and |y|−1 if x=P, treating the unmatched alternative as zero. Let e be the largest sum of these savings over P,Q. Then 1+max(d,c−e) is a lower bound, since H itself was not counted in either child. Minimize over the two gate types and all cuts. This is a relaxation, so a lower bound from the recurrence applies to every hidden tree.

merge-all-slices.cjs evaluates the recurrence over the 8,191 nonempty point subsets. audit-merge-count.cjs independently calculates the largest discount by grouping equal slices and subtracting overlap, then compares all eleven nontrivial size histograms. Both yield D(all points)=8. The minimum by cardinality from zero to thirteen is 0,0,0,0,0,1,1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8. In a circuit of size 25, thirteen terminal ANDs and at least five hidden ORs leave at most seven hidden ANDs, so a connected hidden tree is excluded. The lower bound of five ORs uses the separate argument and finite computation in ../plane920-search/branch-game.md.

## The rest of the 25-gate case

hidden-components.md and its enumeration leave a disconnected graph with two blocks, of which the smaller has at most three points. Separate witnesses and lower bounds in ../plane920-search cover these remaining cuts. The large block independently supplies the target slices of lines crossing between blocks. For a singleton smaller block the four disjoint three-point slices require eight dual ANDs even with OR free. For a two-point or collinear three-point smaller block a 3-by-3 grid of triple outputs needs eleven gates. For three noncollinear points the distinct required pair and triple slices force nine AND gates in the large block, which can then contain at most one OR; the single-OR bound rules out its six triple targets. The finite UNSAT certificates validate particular encodings; these encodings, the case reductions and the tree recurrence still require a Lean bridge. These results together give a mathematical/computational lower bound of 26. They do not prove the posted target of 39.
