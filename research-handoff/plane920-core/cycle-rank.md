# Using cycles in an output's ancestor cone

Use the dual domain and isolate every output as a sink, as in pencil-bound.md. Simplify away constant and unary identity gates by replacing uses with their values or earlier representatives. Discard dead gates. A minimum counterexample may be assumed to have no constant gate. Collapse repeated binary inputs to an identity, so in the finite DAG no vertex has two parallel edges from a single parent. This transformation preserves outputs and does not increase the number of gates. All output cones contain their required input points.

A clean accepting parse for L has no repeated raw point. It uses precisely the four points of L, since otherwise the same parse accepts a proper subset of L. Select one branch at OR and both at AND. A clean parse charges three different binary ANDs to L: these are the branching vertices in the four-leaf binary tree after suppressing unary steps. For such an AND with subtree point-set T and outside context L minus T, replace the subtree by a globally minimum-cardinality set U activating that AND. Since T itself suffices, |U| ≤ |T|. The output accepts U union (L minus T), an admissible set. This enforces U=T. Every global minimum works, so T is unique, has at least two elements and determines L. The three AND gates of one clean parse are distinct, since reuse of a gate with nonempty support under the same assignments can be unfolded with a consistent branch choice; alternatively a repeated gate in the given clean parse would produce disjoint T,T' for that gate and uniqueness would force T=T'. Thus the charges cannot coincide across lines. Equivalently use the structural lemma in ../plane920-math/structural-subcases.md. Every other line still contributes its distinct terminal gate, as a target of one line is inactive on all other line rows.

One may equivalently avoid terminal isolation altogether. For each L choose a single accepting proof DAG D_L at the fixed row L, selecting one true operand per reached OR consistently by gate index, and both operands of a reached AND. Its raw support equals L. Every gate in D_L is active on L, so the other twelve line outputs are absent. Four such DAGs for a pencil have a connected union containing all thirteen points. In the argument below one may use these fixed DAGs throughout: an acyclic D_L unfolds cleanly, and every bad D_L is cyclic. Their edges keep the original circuit directions, so a common cycle has the same merge for every bad D_L. The smaller proof graph suffices for the substitution argument; any accepting assignment for its merge may be inserted. Independent review verified this version as well.

If the total circuit has m ≤ 21 gates, each pencil's ancestor union contains at most m-9 ≤ 12 gates. Its connected underlying undirected graph has at least 13+m_p vertices and at most 2m_p edges, where m_p is the number of gates in the union. It is a tree. Consequently every individual output admits a clean accepting parse. This would force 39 gates. In particular every correct circuit has at least 22 gates.

## The unicyclic case

For m ≤ 22 the cycle rank (edges minus vertices plus one) in every pencil union is at most one. Call a line bad if it has no clean accepting parse. The ancestor cone of a bad line contains an undirected cycle. Every pair of distinct lines belongs to a common pencil, so the undirected cycle of every bad cone is the very same cycle. Otherwise some pencil union would have cycle rank at least two.

In the ancestor cone of a designated output every vertex other than that output has outdegree at least one. In a connected unicyclic graph the number of edges equals its number of vertices. Hence exactly one vertex has outdegree two and all other nonsink vertices have outdegree one. Following the two outgoing paths, they first meet at a vertex t before or at the sink. The two arcs are the unique undirected cycle, and its orientation fixes their common merge t in every bad cone. Removing the two arcs except at t leaves each branch outside the ancestry of t singly connected toward the output. In particular a raw point in the ancestry of t cannot also have a route to the output bypassing t: an additional splitter with two distinct outgoing edges, or a third outgoing edge of the existing splitter, would be needed.

For any bad line L, take an accepting parse at L. It must use t: without t no reconvergence remains on the route to the output. The portion between t and the output has no repeated vertex. Treat t as a single leaf; the other raw leaves have support C disjoint from the raw points in the ancestry of t. Let T be an inclusion-minimal subset of the raw points of that ancestry within L that activates t. Substituting T at the leaf activates the output on T union C, hence this union is L. Now choose any globally minimum-cardinality activating set U for t on the promised domain. Since |U| ≤ |T|, the union U union C is admissible, and makes the same output active. Therefore U union C=L. As |U| ≤ |T| and T,C are disjoint, it follows that U=T. The minimum activating set of t is thus unique and is contained in every bad line. It is nonempty because an active monotone wire on the empty set would be constant and was simplified away.

A nonempty point set is contained in at most four lines. Hence there are at most four bad lines if m ≤ 22, including the cases of no bad line or just one. At least nine lines have clean parses. These nine contribute at least 27 gates and the other lines contribute their terminal gates. More precisely the lower bound is 3(13-b)+b ≥31, where b ≤4. This contradicts m ≤22 and gives a general mathematical lower bound of 23. The result does not have an end-to-end Lean proof.
