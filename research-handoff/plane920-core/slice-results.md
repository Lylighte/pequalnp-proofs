# Free-OR projection tests on eight and nine points

For a fixed coordinate subset U and a projective line L, freeze every coordinate outside U to its value on L. The residual target accepts only the slice S=L intersect U on inputs of size at most |S|. In the tested simultaneous local model, raw variables and AND outputs are atoms. Each counted helper is an AND of two arbitrary disjunctions of earlier atoms. Each target receives one additional free AND of two atoms selected separately per target. A possible free OR on either terminal operand can be narrowed to one atom true on S, since narrowing only removes other positives. Helpers can be omitted or padded. Size-zero, one and two slices impose no constraints in these tests. The model itself does not assert that freezing different lines preserves a shared helper library in arbitrary circuits. The ordinary tree projection and the separately justified fixed-extra-product projection are the applications.

All 1,287 eight-point subsets and all 715 nine-point subsets require at least five helper ANDs in this model. Each size has three orbits under the 5,616 explicitly checked incidence-preserving permutations. Each representative has a full-domain CNF refutation with four helper ANDs. Reproduce the full checksum, orbit and LRAT audit using tools/node/node.exe state/plane920-core/audit-slices.cjs from the project root. It regenerates the original CNFs, expands the archived proofs, independently checks every indicated RUP addition through the empty clause and confirms each subset is accounted for exactly once. Totals are 473,461 and 856,201 added clauses respectively. Independently, slice-controls.cjs forces a direct circuit for each representative and confirms all six complete CNFs are satisfiable at the corresponding higher helper budgets. The audit and controls write their detailed per-orbit receipts under slice-runs. All earlier unknown results for four helpers on nine points are superseded. Trials on ten points remain incomplete . A further projection of an arbitrary local program can freeze outside operands differently for different targets. Extending a lower bound to a new subset size requires a separate transfer proof.
