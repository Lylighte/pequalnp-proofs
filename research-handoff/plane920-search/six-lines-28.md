# Six prescribed line outputs after deleting three OR gates

The input domain consists of all 1,093 sets of at most four of the thirteen points. A prescribed line output must accept precisely its own four-point set on this domain. Each such output is an AND sink, separate from the helper budget. Gates at unprescribed outputs are never read by the original circuit and may be discarded.

| Number of OR gates allowed | Number of nonterminal AND gates allowed | Prescribed outputs |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 10 | 6 |
| 3 | 9 | 6 |
| 4 | 8 | 6 |

All three rows are impossible. The exhaustive four-OR test and its hypotheses are described below.

If an OR operand forces at least two raw points, bypass its OR to the other operand. Take the earliest such OR. The offending operand cannot be raw or an earlier OR, since both children of the latter would likewise force the pair. Its generating hidden AND can be set to zero after discarding the unique possible target line through the pair. The remaining prescribed positives see no change, and the monotone circuit can only decrease on negatives. This reduces the three rows respectively to five lines with budgets (1 OR, 9 AND), (2 OR, 8 AND), or (3 OR, 7 AND). The first is excluded by the single-OR theorem in ../plane920-math/strict-one-or.md. The other two are excluded by five-lines-27.md.

If no such operand occurs, flatten each OR operand as a conjunction stopped at previous named ORs. The raw factors collectively consist of at most one distinct point. The first useful OR is thus the union of two distinct raw points, which can be normalized to points 0 and 1. The 72 point-line permutations fixing this pair give 19 two-OR function orbits and 4,483 three-OR function orbits. Permuting the six prescribed lines does not affect the question, as the test takes the weakest bound over all six-element sets.

For two and three ORs, apply the full-domain shared-AND library and private-AND charging in five-lines-few-ors.md. Every shared candidate can be replaced in the output-cone factorization by a stronger function that preserves the exact positive incidence of the candidate on all thirteen lines. Thus a library element is charged to a distinct physical AND. Shared ANDs whose strengthened function equals an already free atom are allowed. If the physical circuit has no shared ANDs at all, charging the two nonterminal ANDs in each individual positive proof already gives twelve distinct gates. The two-OR run needs the library test for 1 out of 19 function orbits. The three-OR run needs it for 615 out of 4,483 orbits. Both have zero survivors. Outputs are in few-or-2-6-10.json and few-or-3-6-9.json.

## Extra restrictions at four ORs and eight auxiliary ANDs

A prescribed line cannot have two ANDs private to it, where private means true on that line and false on all other prescribed lines. Deleting both gates and the output at that line would compute five prescribed lines with four ORs and six hidden ANDs, excluded in five-lines-27.md. This also bounds the number of ANDs private on the full thirteen geometric line inputs.

If an OR arm is computed by a hidden AND and is true on at most one prescribed line, bypass the OR to its other arm and set the AND to zero. Discarding at most that one output leaves five complete lines with at most three ORs and seven hidden ANDs, which is impossible. We only use the weaker test on all thirteen line inputs. A flattened operand whose full function is neither a raw point nor an earlier OR must have a physical hidden AND at its root. Functions equal to such a free signal are always kept. This test is invariant under the geometric symmetries. It reduces the three-OR prefixes to 1,897 orbits. Distinct fourth-OR operand decompositions giving the same truth vector on the domain are combined only after all operand-level restrictions have passed.

If one arm's positive line set contains the other's, the OR can be replaced by the dominating arm, preserving all prescribed positives. This gives six lines at three ORs and eight hidden ANDs, already excluded. Otherwise perform a first test at every line L. There can be at most one private AND, hence the terminal AND and its private cone combine at most three boundary factors. Every boundary factor is a raw point, a named OR, or a flattened AND true on another line and hence containing at most one raw point. Thus some triple of raw points in L conjoined with all named ORs true on L must isolate L on the domain. This preliminary test can be checked by intersecting the sets of erroneous inputs independently with each arm of the fourth OR. At least six lines must pass.

For survivors form the exact all-possible-shared per-line relaxation and then enumerate the possible paid libraries as above. For each selected family T of six lines require its library-relative lower costs to be at most one each. For each nonempty J contained in T of size one, two, or three, discard every private gate charged to a line outside J and one distinct physical AND for each selected library mask missing all of J. Replacing those gates with zero preserves the original values on J by induction on the gate order and cannot introduce a false positive. For one, two, and three prescribed lines the surviving circuit respectively needs at least two, four, and five hidden ANDs even with free ORs. The two-line case uses the free-OR RUP proof with 7,024 additions at ../plane920-math/small-core/deleted/family-3-3-s-20000.json. The three-line bound has the two incidence-type RUP proofs at ../plane920-math/small-core/deleted/family-{7,35}-4-s-125000.json. This deletion test charges distinct gates only: selected library gates were active on multiple geometric lines and charged private gates were active on exactly one.

The finite checks use JavaScript integer masks and full-domain truth tables, and have not been formalized in Lean. To reproduce in this directory use the bundled Node executable with three-or-five-lines.cjs 2 6 10, three-or-five-lines.cjs 3 6 9, and four-or-six-lines.cjs 0 1897 --shared-arms.

The complete four-OR run in four-or-six-shared-0-1897.json enumerates 1,675,184 final arm pairs. Of 1,477,787 positive-incomparable pairs, 163,582 pass the preliminary per-line test. The 160,704 distinct prefix-plus-fourth-OR domain functions among these yield 155,856 finite-library checks. The histogram of preliminary minimum private sums is 14 at three gates, 802 at four, 22,909 at five, and 132,131 at six. Every library is excluded. The run took 152.9 seconds. The independent pointwise test in six-lines-crosscheck.cjs reconstructs 1,144 fourth ORs across nineteen prefixes and compares 29,744 two- and three-raw-factor predicates, including 4,076 positives, with the split-arm test and the exact private bound. All comparisons passed.

Consequently, if resolving the first three OR gates of a 28-gate instance with five, six, or seven ORs jointly preserves six full line positives, the reduced circuit contradicts the relevant row. The exceptional three-OR patterns still require their own treatment in the full instance.
