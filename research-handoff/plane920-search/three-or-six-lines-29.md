# A three-OR circuit cannot save two hidden ANDs on five or six complete lines

For k equal to five or six, k prescribed full plane-line indicators on the weight-at-most-four domain require at least 2k−1 hidden ANDs if at most three ORs are available. Each output has a separate uncharged AND terminal. The four-target base case at the same threshold is in five-lines-seven-helpers.md. The following finite argument also classifies the two-OR circuits at a one-AND saving that it uses.

## Local normal form for small private budgets

Call a hidden AND private to a selected output if it is true on that line and false on every other selected line. A gate false on all selected positives can be removed by substitution of false. If a line has two private gates, deleting that line and setting both gates to false preserves the other outputs. Thus a smallest-k three-OR counterexample saving two ANDs has at most one private gate per selected line. The same argument can be used to peel a two-OR circuit saving one AND.

With at most one private gate per line, flatten any active wire up to earlier named ORs. Every shared hidden AND has at most one explicit raw factor because distinct positive lines intersect in one point. An active private AND cannot have a private AND among its two immediate inputs. Such an input would have to be private to the very same line. Its two inputs are therefore raw, named ORs, or shared ANDs. The complete private function is a conjunction of at most two explicit raw factors and some earlier named ORs. In particular every OR operand admits this representation. An OR with an operand forcing two raw points can be bypassed to the opposite operand, discarding the unique selected line where that operand is true. At the earliest such OR its operand is an actual hidden AND. That gate can also be set to false. This is the rare-arm deletion used below.

## Tight circuits using two ORs

With two ORs, k=4 or 5 requires at least 2k−1 helpers by the lower bounds in five-lines-27.md. For k=3 the corresponding five-helper bound holds even with free ORs. At equality, having at least two helpers private to one line permits peeling exactly two helpers and that line. Both ORs and all remaining helpers must survive: one OR needs 2(k−1) helpers by the strict one-OR theorem. No peeled helper can directly feed a surviving internal gate, since such a gate would become zero or an OR could be bypassed.

To exclude an unpeelable tight circuit, enumerate all two-OR functions when every line has at most one private gate. The first OR is the disjunction of two different monomials of size one or two. These yield five symmetry orbits. The second OR uses the local normal form above. The 4,024 ordered function classes resulting from successive stabilizers cover all positive-incomparable choices, without choosing a target family. A positively dominated OR can be bypassed on all prescribed rows; this would leave the impossible one-OR case.

For each candidate line, three raw points times all named ORs true there must be sufficient to isolate the line on all 1,093 rows. This follows by the two-operand expansion of a terminal with at most one private AND. Of the 4,024 classes, only 979 can cover four lines in this relaxation and 104 can cover five. For each physical decomposition charge every distinct nonfree OR operand. Enumerate additional shared libraries by their exact positive mask as in six-lines-28.md. On each selected family charge a required singleton-library gate to its target when checking the cap of one private helper. Every original OR must have at least one exclusively active row in each of its two arms within that family, since otherwise one OR suffices. The number of charged decompositions tested is 531 for four targets and 73 for five. Only the same class numbered 678 survives either test. Its second OR's nonraw arm has function (raw mask 192) AND first-OR. Enumerating its two physical AND inputs with the local normal form gives six proper decompositions. After paying for their nonfree operands no family survives any of them.

Consequently, every tight four- or five-target two-OR computation can be peeled down to a tight three-target computation. There the complete search in five-lines-seven-helpers.md gives two normalized choices for the ordered physical OR inputs, and six sets of five physical AND signals. Every peeling preserves both ORs and their input wires. In particular the two ORs in the original four- or five-target circuit are each an independent disjunction of one bare point and one pure bare-point pair, in the same incidence configuration as those of a three-target core.

## Three ORs with a two-helper saving

If no OR arm forces two raw points, each arm has at most one explicit raw point. At five targets/eight helpers and six targets/ten helpers, the standard 4,483 normalized three-OR function orbits leave four candidates each after the initial shared-library check. Each allows just one set of actual nonfree operand functions. Charging those functions rules out all four candidates in either case. See paid-three-5-8.json and paid-three-6-10.json.

Otherwise choose the earliest OR X having an operand H forcing two raw points, with other operand B. Work with the smallest counterexample k in {5,6}, using the proved four-target base. It has at most one private helper per selected line. Deleting X, H and the only possible target of H gives a tight two-OR circuit on k−1 targets. If H were false on all selected targets, removing any one line would still give the tight model; alternatively bypassing X preserves all targets and already violates the two-OR lower bound. The tight circuit contains the two physical ORs of a three-target core as proved above. If X had a predecessor OR, the first OR would be unchanged by the deletion and would already contain the core's bare-pair arm, contradicting earliest choice. Thus X is the first OR, H a pure raw pair, and B a raw singleton or pair. H cannot feed a surviving gate except X, since deleting H would remove an extra gate from the tight residual circuit.

Normalize the three residual core targets to mask 7. Take each of the two possible ordered pairs of residual ORs. Insert X first. Its pure-pair H must be false on the residual three positives and true on an extra target. For each of the next two ORs enumerate both original operands in the local two-raw-factor normal form. Evaluate this representation once using the real earlier ORs and once substituting B for X and the fixed residual values for intervening ORs. Accept only representations with exactly the required residual input function on every allowed input. This substitution preserves the actual residual inputs because the eliminated H has no other surviving successors. A physical decomposition with the same value on the promise is allowed in the enumeration.

The complete restoration considers 9,720 choices of H,B over the two core types and 10,824 completed original prefixes. For each selected family require both sides of every OR to be exclusively true somewhere. Every required operand that is private to a selected line exhausts that line's private allowance. Its terminal must either be accessible with zero private factors, or must use this operand directly as a boundary input together with at most one raw point and all applicable named OR factors: no private AND can intervene before the next OR or terminal. These tests leave 80 five-target families for final library charging, all rejected. For six targets zero families reach final charging. All truth comparisons use the full promised domain. The programme counts and outcomes are in lift-three-or-kernel.json.

The two new bounds show that any 29-gate instance with six dual ORs that retains six lines after resolving its first three ORs is impossible. These are finite local calculations. A Lean proof of the statement on the desk would require additional arguments and formalization.
